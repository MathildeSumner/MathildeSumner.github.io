My Favorite Object

+

August 24, 2026

In our first class session, we discussed our favorite objects, and I chose my childhood blanket. My family was in Hurricane Katrina and we lost all of my baby stuff, and someone at the Red Cross gave it to me, which is what makes it so special to me. It has always been something that brings me a lot of comfort and reminds me of my family. My parents even found another one on eBay and have kept it just in case anything happened to mine.

The blanket is a Winnie the Pooh baby blanket and is made from a very soft, plush fabric. I believe the fabric is called minky, which is very soft and has a slightly fuzzy texture. The softness of the material is a big part of why the blanket is so comforting to me. There is also a darker blue felt border around the blanket which has held it together for many years.

It suprised me how hard it was to use these hand tools and it has given me a greater apreciation for the people who use them. I had only used power tools in the past when woodworking so this was suprisingly difficult. Picture Frame: Part 2

+

September 10, 2026

I made this picture frame out of walnut wood and I oiled it to make it shiny. This one definitly came out better than the first frame but there are definitly some things that I would do differently if I did it again. When I was cutting the wood with the sled I realized when I was almost done that I hadent been pushing me wood all the way to the bottom before I clamped it to the sled so some of my cuts were slightly uneven. I also had some trouble getting the joints to fit together and overcompensated by cutting off too much wood and there were some gaps after. I feel much more confident with the table saw and the sled after doing this project and think that if I was to do it one more time it would be much better. The rabbets were also a bit trickey becasue I couldn't figure out a way to not cut all the way through on the side pieces and have it just be a pocket. So I just cut all the way through and it looks fine to me, if I was to do it again I might use a dremmal to cut that part out but the rabbets still work like they are supposed to. I also cut out a clear piece of acrylic to put in front of my picture for some stability and then hammered in two small pieces of wood in the corners in order to hold everything in place. Overall im quite happy with how it all came out and definitly learned a lot from this project

Cardboard Couture: Shoes

+

September 15, 2026

Making this show was a lot of fun! I made a replica of my adidas sambas with ruffles as the signiture stripes. I picked these shoes just becasue I thought the corrigation could look really cool as the ruffles. I started off by tracing the shoe and making 3 layers for the base with a thin strip along the outside to give it a clean look. I made the upper of the shoe multiple times and ripped it off to start again multiple times, the shoe box gave me a lot of trouble. I ended up making to upper in 3 parts, the ankle/back, the middle, and the shoe box. Once I was done making the foundation of the shoe, adding the little details was the most fun part of making it. Making the tongue and laces was a bit complicated becasue I was worried I would make it to far into the shoe and might not be able to fit my foot in. Overall i'm very happy with how this came out, once I started adding the details I got into a flow state and wanted to go all out. I added extra ruffles around the edge for fun and also colored some of the details with a pink sharpie which I think really took it to another level. I was also able to successfully walk in it with no issues and looked super cool in my new cardboard shoe.

#9 American Elm

I liked this tree, it was large and had lots of leaves. American elms were historically planted along streets across America because of their large, shady canopies. Unfortunately, many were killed by Dutch elm disease. They are not native to Colorado, but grow here. American elm is a hardwood and has been used for furniture, flooring, and even boats because its wood is tough and resists splitting. It also holds up pretty well when wet.

#21 Chestnut Oak

This one was also a very nice tree. Its thick, dark gray bark contains a lot of tannins, which were historically used for tanning leather. Chestnut oak is native to the eastern United States, not Colorado. It is a hardwood and has been used for railroad ties, flooring, and furniture because it is strong and durable. It was also named Urban Tree of the Year in 2017.

#51 Ponderosa Pine

This one was very skinny and tall. If you put your nose right up to the bark, it smells like vanilla or butterscotch, which I thought was really cool. Ponderosa pine is native to Colorado and has historically been an important source of lumber throughout the western United States. It is a softwood and is commonly used for framing, doors, windows, and furniture because it is relatively lightweight and easy to work with. These trees are also really good at surviving wildfires because of their thick bark.

#36 Silver Maple

This was my favorite of the trees we saw today. The leaves were very nice and pretty. Silver maples are native to eastern and central North America, but not Colorado. They were historically planted in cities because they grow quickly and provide lots of shade. Silver maple is technically a hardwood, although its wood is softer than many other maples. It can be used for furniture, cabinets, and interior woodwork, but it is not usually used for structural building because the wood is relatively weak. They are also some of the first trees to flower in spring.
